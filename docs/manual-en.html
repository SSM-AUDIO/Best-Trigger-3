1. About

Best Trigger 3 is a drum trigger. It finds every hit on a recorded kick, snare or tom track and plays your samples instead of the original or together with it.

  • Up to six samples at once, each with level, pan, tune, envelope, filters, Mute/Solo and phase flip.
  • Multisamples: velocity layers and round-robin takes, the .bt3 format.
  • An oscilloscope with a draggable threshold, Retrigger and sample Offsets.
  • Phase tools and Full Auto.
  • A | B, Undo/Redo, presets, a sample browser, MIDI in and out.

2. Installation

Requirements: Windows 10/11 (64-bit) or macOS 10.15 and newer; a host (DAW) that loads VST3 plugins.

Windows

  1. Download the installer from download page and run it. If Windows shows a blue warning, click More info → Run anyway.
  2. Close your DAW first and rescan plugins afterwards.
  3. Insert the plugin on a drum track as an effect.

macOS

macOS warns you the first time: see how to allow the plugin.

3. Trial and licence

You can try Best Trigger 3 for 14 days with all its functions, or enter a licence key you bought (how to buy). Internet is needed once for either.

The trial

  1. Insert the plugin. Its window offers Start 14-day trial: press it.
  2. All functions are open for 14 days. The bottom right corner shows how many days are left.
  3. The trial is one per computer: deleting the plugin or reinstalling the system does not start it again.

The licence

  1. Type your key XXXXX-XXXXX-XXXXX-XXXXX in the window and press Activate. It works at any time, also during the trial.
  2. The bottom right corner then says Licensed.
  • One key, one computer. To move the licence press Deactivate this computer in the licence window, then activate on the new one.
  • The plugin works offline and checks the licence from time to time when online.
  • Usage statistics are sent only if you agree (gear → Help improve Best Trigger). Your audio is never sent.

When the trial has ended

The licence window stays over the plugin until you enter a key. Your project is not changed: the sound passes through, but no samples are played, and every 15 seconds the plugin hisses for about four seconds. Enter the key and everything works again at once.

4. Quick start

  1. Insert the plugin on the drum track as an effect (see Installation).
  2. Press Start 14-day trial or enter your key (see Trial and licence).
  3. Put samples in: drop a file on a plate, right-click the plate, or open the Browser. A click on a plate plays it (further right = harder).
  4. To hear the original too, raise the Original fader to 0 dB (it starts at −∞).
  5. Start playback and choose Full Auto (button in the right panel): the plugin listens to the music and sets the threshold, the phase of the samples and their levels.
  6. Look at the result on the scope: Timeline (the hits), Phase (the last hit), Spectrum (the frequency response).

Separate outputs (REAPER)

  1. Add the plugin to a track. Right-click its name in the FX chain → Build multichannel routing for output of selected FX: REAPER creates a track for every output.
  2. In the plugin press OUT on the channels you want on their own tracks (the original and Smp 1 … Smp 6).
  3. Such a channel plays on its own track and leaves the main Output.

Separate outputs in other DAWs, and Mac

Separate outputs are extra output buses of the plugin. They are tested in REAPER only. Other VST3 hosts (Cubase, Ableton Live, Studio One and others) may or may not offer such buses for an effect plugin. If you do not see them, use the main Output: everything plays there as long as no OUT button is on.

Logic Pro and Pro Tools do not load VST3 plugins (Logic uses only Audio Units, Pro Tools only AAX), so Best Trigger 3 cannot be used in them at the moment.

Mac and trackpad: where this manual says Ctrl + click or drag, use Cmd on a Mac (a Ctrl + click there is a right click; the right click is a two-finger tap). On the Spectrum view drag the picture with the mouse or with two fingers; Cmd + scroll stretches the curve. In Timeline with Hold, a sideways two-finger scroll replaces the middle-button drag.

Presets

A preset stores all settings and loaded samples. The Presets button opens the list; use Save, Save as…, Delete and Initialize (default settings). The arrows step through presets, and Undo takes a loaded preset back.

6. Hit detection

Set these on the scope in the Timeline view. Drag a value box up or down to change it (Shift = finer; the wheel over it changes it a little); a double-click lets you type a number.

ParameterMeaningDefault
ThresholdThe level above which the signal is a hit. Drag the red lines. If the threshold of the Sensitivity (below) is higher, that one decides.−12 dB
Sensitivity0–100. The lower it is, the louder a hit has to be to be noticed: it sets a threshold of its own (very high at 0–5, about −6 dB at 10, about −22 dB at 50, about −41 dB at 100). The higher of this threshold and the Threshold decides. Drag the box up or down, or double-click and type.50
RetriggerThe dead time after a hit. Drag the edge of the purple zone, or the Retrigger box up or down.40 ms
InputHow a hit is turned into a velocity: the velocity is proportional to the peak of the hit, and a hit that peaks at the Input level is velocity 127 (the loudest layer). The default (−6 dB) is the scale of the well-known trigger: layers and the level inside a layer behave as they do there. Full Auto sets it to your loudest hits; or drag the green Loud line. The Threshold is only where a hit is noticed.−6 dB
Low Cut / High CutFilters for the detector only; the sound does not change.—

Dynamics: 1.00 = the hit strength fully controls the layer and loudness; 0 = always the hardest layer at full level.

  1. Start playback; the wheel zooms the time window, Hold freezes the picture; the height fits itself.
  2. Drag the red lines so that real hits cross them and unwanted sounds stay below.
  3. Crosses mark sounds skipped by Retrigger. Real hits crossed out: lower Retrigger. Several lines on one hit: raise Retrigger or the Threshold.
Tip. With a lot of bleed from other drums, put a gate before Best Trigger.

7. Samples and mixer

On every hit all loaded samples play at once.

  • drop a file on a plate, or right-click the plate: Load sample or .bt3 file…, Load folder (multisample)…, Save multisample file (.bt3)…, Copy sample and Paste sample (the same sample into another channel), Rename… (the name on the plate; it is saved with the project), Copy sample name (name and extension, to the clipboard), Clear sample. WAV, AIFF, FLAC.
  • Drag a plate onto another plate: the two samples swap places (Ctrl/Cmd held: a copy is put there and the first stays). Levels, pan and the other settings stay with the channels.
  • Ctrl/Cmd + click on plates, or a frame drawn over the empty part of the mixer, selects samples (white border). In the Phase view, dragging the Offset of one selected sample moves all the selected ones by the same amount. A click on an empty place clears the selection.
  • A click on a plate plays only that sample with all its settings; further right = harder.
ElementDescription
TickShows or hides the trace on the scope.
FaderLevel, −∞ to +24 dB. Original starts at −∞, samples at 0 dB.
M / SMute and Solo. Right-click M, S or Ø: only this channel gets it, all the others lose it (a quick way to listen to one).
LatencyThe original is delayed by the plugin latency (39 ms; the DAW makes up for it) so that samples can start before it and be moved earlier with a negative Offset.
ØFlips the phase of the sample.
ENVAttack, Sustain and Release of the sample.
EQAn equaliser for this channel, the Original too: Low Cut, Low Shelf, three Bells, High Shelf and High Cut. Drag the dots; the wheel over a bell changes its width (Q); a double click on a dot resets it; a right click on a dot switches that filter off and on and keeps its numbers. The numbers of the selected dot are shown at the bottom left and can be typed (1500, 1.5k, gain in dB). Low Cut and High Cut also have a Slope: 6, 12, 18 or 24 dB per octave (12 is the usual one). No sound? Check: activation, a sample on the plate, Mute/Solo, the fader, and that the Threshold is not too high.

8. Multisamples

A multisample has velocity layers and several round-robin takes. Load a folder: right-click a plate → Load folder (multisample)…. Name the files like Name_R1_V0.wav (Name_V0_R1.wav works too):

  • R + number: the take.
  • V + number: the velocity (0–127) from which the layer plays, e.g. _V0, _V50, _V105.
  • Small numbers (_V1 … _V4) are spread evenly; the plugin checks which end is the loudest recording, so it does not matter which number is the hardest hit.

To save a multisample as one .bt3 file: right-click the plate → Save multisample file (.bt3)….

9. Library and browser

  • Library folder: gear → Sample library. By default Documents\SSM-Audio\Best Trigger 3\Library.
  • Browser (right panel): a tree of your library with a search box (type to search, the cross clears it) and Audition. A click plays a file; a double-click loads it into the next free channel; you can also drag onto a pad. Right-click a file for more, right-click a pad to clear it. A sound file can also be dragged from the browser straight into the DAW.
  • Remember place (bottom left): the browser opens where you stopped. WAV and BT3 files have different icon colours.
  • Online libraries…: free libraries with a cover and the author's text. Choose one and press Download and install.
  • WAV to .bt3…: turns WAV files into .bt3 files (one file per sample, smaller and quicker to load): the selected file or folder, or the whole library. The .bt3 is put next to the WAV; existing ones are never overwritten. Afterwards you are asked whether to delete the source WAV files (they go to the Recycle Bin).
  • Migration…: converts a .tci library into .bt3 files. Choose the folder; the files are created in your library. Convert only free samples or your own.
  • Converted .tci instruments are played at the sample rate written in the file and at the volumes written in it (how loud each layer plays at its lowest and highest velocity), exactly as the well-known trigger plays them. Instruments converted by an earlier version are converted again from their .tci when you run the conversion again.

10. Phase

Next to each sample name is a role button: LEAD (lined up with the original; the first LEAD sample is the leader), FOL (moves together with the leader: rooms and overheads) and ALIGN (finds its own offset and polarity against the leader, without music: Out, Sub). Samples of one set also play the same take on every hit.

A sample played with the original can thin out the sound if their waves cancel. Press the Phase button on the scope to see the last hit: the original, the samples and their sum.

  1. Click a Smp box at the top of the scope and drag the graph left or right to move that sample's Offset (±30 ms; Shift = finer, double-click the graph = 0), or drag the box up or down. With several samples selected, they all move together.
  2. If the first waves go in opposite directions, press Ø.
  3. Watch Sum gain: green (+1.5 dB or more) is good, red (below −1.5 dB) means the channels cancel.

Auto → Track phase on every hit fine-tunes the start of each sample by up to ±2 ms. It works best on a clean track.

11. Auto

The main tools have buttons of their own in the right panel: Full Auto, Auto Threshold and Auto Phase. The Auto button opens a menu with the others. While a tool listens to the music, its button blinks. Every result can be undone with Undo.

Whole signal / BassThe part of the signal used for the analysis. Bass for a kick, whole signal for a snare.

The tools need sound: start playback.

12. MIDI

  • Output: every hit is also sent as a MIDI note (gear → MIDI out note).
  • Recording: press REC MIDI, play, press it again, then drag the green drag MIDI clip to DAW handle onto a MIDI track.
  • Input: gear → Trigger source → MIDI and a MIDI in note; the velocity sets the hit strength.
